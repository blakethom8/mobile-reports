Live Betting Architecture — Mini Report

Prediction Market Weather · March 22, 2026

What changed

The project focus has shifted from primarily building historical research plumbing to building a point-in-time betting architecture for live-style paper trading.

The new goal is to behave as if we are operating the strategy in real time:

  • see the available bets for the day
  • compare them across cities
  • decide the day’s strategy before picking any one bet
  • record rationale and forecast context
  • settle outcomes and review results

Core operating loop

1. Strategy session2. Market board3. Paper bet4. Outcome review

  • Strategy session: define the day, focus cities, and thesis.
  • Market board: gather the candidate markets and compare their edge side by side.
  • Paper bet: simulate the trade only after the board-level strategy is clear.
  • Outcome review: log realized P&L, result, and lessons learned.

New architecture now in the repo

  • ops.strategy_sessions — one record for the day’s strategy context
  • ops.strategy_market_board — captured candidate markets for that strategy session
  • ops.paper_bets — simulated bets with rationale and later outcome
  • features.v_latest_market_training_rows — latest row per market
  • features.v_daily_market_board — candidate ranking / bucketing view
  • weatherlab.ops.strategy — create strategy sessions and populate the board
  • weatherlab.ops.paper_bets — create and settle paper bets

Why this matters

This structure lets us improve the system even before the full ML layer is mature. We can learn from:

  • which bets we chose
  • which bets we passed on
  • how the forecast compared to the market
  • whether our edge selection process is producing small, repeatable gains
The important shift is: we are no longer evaluating bets one-by-one in isolation. We are building toward a daily cross-market selection process that looks for relative alpha.

How ML fits later

ML is still part of the long-term architecture, but now it becomes a component inside the decision framework, not the whole framework itself.

  • today: use forecasts, board comparison, and disciplined rationale capture
  • later: add ML fair probabilities / confidence estimates into the same board
  • eventually: let an LLM decision layer read the board, compare opportunities, and recommend or simulate bets

Recommended next steps

  • build a day-of workflow to populate the board for tomorrow’s live markets
  • capture abstentions, not just bets
  • add a simple daily strategy summary artifact
  • add cross-city ranking rules and exposure constraints
  • only then deepen the ML layer into the same operating system

# Brand Strategy — Three Directions

*Companion to `00-stakeholder-synthesis.md`. Each direction is a complete, self-consistent brand hypothesis executed as a marketing site + advertising suite in its own folder.*

---

## Shared Foundation (true in all three directions)

- **Category claim:** The eldercare operating system for families — AI agents for the legwork, humans for trust, across medical, physical, and financial care.
- **Primary wedge:** 2–5 distributed adult siblings co-managing an aging parent.
- **Message pillars:** (1) One place for the whole picture. (2) Built for families, not just caregivers. (3) AI that does the legwork. (4) Humans when it matters. (5) Trust by design.
- **Pricing:** Connected $79/mo · Supported $149/mo · Managed $299/mo, plus income-scaled pricing and scholarships.
- **Moral constraint:** The parent's dignity is non-negotiable in imagery, copy, and product.
- **North star:** Help one million families with long-term longevity care.

## Direction A — **Harbor** · "The calm hand on the shoulder"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Personality** | Warm, dignified, steady. Headspace meets One Medical. |
| **Audience center** | The exhausted primary coordinator (Sarah). D2C-first. |
| **Tagline candidates** | "Care for the people who care." · "A safe harbor for your family's hardest season." · "Everything about Mom's care, in one calm place." |
| **Voice** | Reassuring, plain-spoken, emotionally literate. Short sentences. Never clinical, never salesy. |
| **Visual system** | Cream `#f7f4ee`, soft white `#fffdf9`, sage `#8fae9d`, teal-blue `#7ea9b3`, deep trust blue `#4e7a86`, warm sand `#d7c1a6`, ink `#23313a`. Serif headlines (Georgia), sans body (Inter/system). Rounded containers, generous whitespace. |
| **Risk** | Can read as soft/passive; must keep operational competence visible. |

## Direction B — **Trellis** · "The family's chief of staff"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Personality** | Modern, confident, quietly cool. Linear/Notion polish applied to a deeply human problem. The direction that makes a 45-year-old tech-comfortable buyer say "finally, someone serious." |
| **Audience center** | The capable-but-drowning sibling team; skews slightly younger/urban. D2C with strong product-led energy. |
| **Tagline candidates** | "Run your parents' care like it matters." · "The operating system for family care." · "From group-text chaos to one command center." |
| **Voice** | Crisp, direct, lightly witty but never flippant. Verbs over adjectives. Shows the product early. |
| **Visual system** | Deep evergreen `#1d3b2f`, warm off-white `#faf8f3`, moss `#6f8f7a`, amber signal `#d9a441`, charcoal ink `#20242a`. Geometric sans throughout (system sans, tight tracking on headlines). Structured grids, visible UI, subtle motion cues. A trellis = the structure that supports growth — the family provides the care, Trellis provides the structure. |
| **Risk** | Too-cool can feel cold at the worst moment of someone's life; must keep human warmth in testimonial and services sections. |

## Direction C — **Meridian** · "Your family's private care office"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Personality** | Quiet luxury, discretion, permanence. A family-office feel for eldercare. What a wealth manager proudly refers and a Medicare Advantage VP puts in a benefits packet. |
| **Audience center** | Affluent ICP 1 + the advisor/health-plan channel (B2B2C). |
| **Tagline candidates** | "The standard of care your family keeps." · "A private care office for your parents." · "Discretion. Coordination. Continuity." |
| **Voice** | Measured, assured, editorial. Longer line lengths, fewer exclamations, zero hype. Speaks about stewardship and continuity. |
| **Visual system** | Deep navy `#16273b`, porcelain `#f6f4ef`, champagne gold `#c2a878`, slate `#5c6b7a`, soft sky `#dce7ee`. Serif display (didone-adjacent stack), refined sans body. Thin rules, small caps, generous margins. |
| **Risk** | Exclusivity can contradict the mission ("one million families," scholarships); the concierge tier framing must coexist with approachability messaging in the health-plan context. |

## Evaluation Criteria (for choosing among directions)

1. Would Sarah trust it at 11pm after a bad phone call about Dad?
2. Would Ruth feel dignified seeing her family use it?
3. Would Marcus co-brand it in a member benefits packet?
4. Would an elder-law attorney refer it without hesitation?
5. Can it hold for 30 years (Epic-style), not 3?

2FA lockouts are a top abandonment moment for this demographic, so the fallback is a phone number that reaches a person — not a help article. The heading explains why we're double-checking; unexplained friction reads as suspicion. Code boxes are large, high-contrast, and auto-advance.

Every dead end offers two live exits — a timed reset link and a human on the phone — plus a nudge toward the passwordless path so this screen is needed less over time.

No passwords for the parent — ever. The default is one huge button and a code to a phone she already holds; the 4-digit PIN is optional and set up together with family or with Grace, never alone. Help routes to a named person ("your daughter Marisol"), not a support queue. Dignity means Elena is never made to feel locked out of her own life.

Why we do this

Transparency beats silent security. In a family, unusual access isn't just a security event — it's a trust event. When everyone can see who signed in, where, and when, suspicion has nowhere to grow, and no one can quietly act in Elena's name without the family knowing.

Bank-level encryption · HIPAA-aligned · You decide who sees what

The family IS the security perimeter. Notifying everyone — including Elena, in her large-print format — converts private suspicion into shared awareness, and it directly protects Elena from financial exploitation, a real elder-abuse vector. The "secure the account" action is warm-warn, not alarm red: decisive without inducing panic.

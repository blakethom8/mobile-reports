How We Should Structure Prediction Market ML Research

Do NOT start with “train a model”

Same lesson as the fraud project: training a model too early is how you produce complicated nonsense. We need a clean research loop first.

Recommended Project Skeleton

prediction-market-lab/ ├── data/ │ ├── raw/ │ │ ├── kalshi/ │ │ ├── polymarket/ │ │ └── external/ │ ├── warehouse/ │ │ ├── prediction_markets.duckdb │ │ └── parquet/ │ └── features/ ├── research/ │ ├── strategies/ │ │ ├── strategy-A-market-microstructure.md │ │ ├── strategy-B-domain-weather.md │ │ ├── strategy-C-domain-macro.md │ │ └── strategy-D-sequence-model.md │ ├── reports/ │ └── notebooks/ ├── src/ │ ├── ingest/ │ ├── labeling/ │ ├── features/ │ ├── models/ │ ├── backtest/ │ └── live/ ├── eval.py ├── signal.py ├── results.tsv └── README.md

The Core Loop

FileRole
eval.pyFixed evaluator. Loads historical market states + outcomes and scores a strategy/model on a strict backtest.
signal.pyEditable logic. Given a market state and optional external features, outputs probability / edge / trade action.
results.tsvEvery run recorded with metrics and description.

That’s the fraud architecture translated into trading research.

What the Fixed Evaluator Should Measure

The fraud detector had AUC/AP. Prediction markets need different metrics.

MetricWhy it matters
Total P&LObvious — did the strategy make money?
ROICapital efficiency
Sharpe-like ratioReturn adjusted for volatility
Max drawdownHow painful / survivable the strategy is
Brier scoreProbability calibration
Log lossPunishes overconfidence
Hit rateUseful but not sufficient
Turnover / trade countOperational realism
Slippage / spread sensitivityPrevents fake backtests
Important: A prediction model is not enough. The evaluator has to simulate actual trading constraints: spread, fees, time-to-resolution, no lookahead leakage, and capital limits. Otherwise we’ll build a paper-rich fantasy strategy.

Recommended Research Tracks

Track 1 — Market Microstructure (Best first baseline)

  • Use only market-native information: price path, spread, volume, open interest, time-to-close
  • Question: when is the market miscalibrated just from its own behavior?
  • Good for both Kalshi and Polymarket

Track 2 — Domain Models (Best real edge)

  • Weather: NOAA/NWS forecasts + market prices
  • Macro: CPI/jobs/GDP leading indicators + market prices
  • This is where your actual edge probably lives

Track 3 — Sequence Model / Karpathy-style

  • Structured token sequence of market states + external inputs
  • Predict future price move, calibration delta, or terminal resolution
  • Do this only after Tracks 1 and 2 have clean baselines

Where the analogy breaks

  • Fraud detection is a static classification problem; prediction markets are time-series + execution
  • Fraud labels are sparse but objective; market edge depends on beating the price, not just predicting the final outcome
  • For trading, calibration versus the current market price matters more than raw classification accuracy

The Most Important Design Choice

Do we train the model to predict final resolution, or to predict mispricing relative to current market price? My strong recommendation: target mispricing / expected edge. Markets already encode a lot of information. The job is not “predict the world” from scratch — it’s “beat the market.”

Recommended Phase Plan

Phase 1 — Warehouse + Backtest

  • Create normalized tables for markets, trades, and resolutions
  • Build eval.py backtester
  • Build a trivial signal.py baseline

Phase 2 — Weather Research Loop

  • Attach NOAA/NWS forecast history
  • Generate features comparing market price to forecast distribution
  • Run repeated experiments, log to results.tsv

Phase 3 — Macro Research Loop

  • Add CPI/jobs/Fed markets
  • Join BLS/BEA/Fed leading indicators
  • Backtest rule-based signals before ML

Phase 4 — Sequence Models

  • Structured model on market state sequences
  • Compare against simple baselines, not against vibes

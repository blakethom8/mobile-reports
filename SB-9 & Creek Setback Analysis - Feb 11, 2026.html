What is SB-9?

California Senate Bill 9 (effective January 1, 2022) is state housing legislation that:

  1. Allows lot splits to create up to two parcels from a single-family lot
  2. Permits up to two units per parcel (main house + ADU or two units)
  3. Mandates ministerial approval by cities (staff-level, no discretionary review)
  4. Limits local government ability to deny projects that meet objective standards

Requirements & Constraints Under SB-9

1. ADU vs. Second Unit Designation

Critical Decision: The size of the accessory structure determines its classification and fee structure.

Classification Size Development Fee Discount Estimated Fee Impact
ADU <1,000 sf 80% discount Base fees
Second Unit ≥1,000 sf No discount +$20,000-$30,000

Current Design: ~1,200 sf (classified as "Second Unit")

Implications:

  • Both classifications have same 4'-0" setback requirements (no difference)
  • Both qualify for SB-9 staff-level review (no difference)
  • Attached Second Unit counts toward Very Large Home calculation (but project has room)
  • Second Unit has 27'-0" height limit (same as primary residence)

Trade-Off Analysis

Option A: Keep ≥1,000 sf (Second Unit)

  • ✅ More space (~200 sf additional)
  • ✅ Better rental/resale value potential
  • ✅ More flexibility for intended use
  • ❌ Additional $20,000-$30,000 in development fees
  • ❌ "Second Unit" designation

Option B: Reduce to <1,000 sf (ADU)

  • ✅ Save $20,000-$30,000 in fees
  • ✅ "ADU" designation (more favorable)
  • ✅ Still qualifies for SB-9 path
  • ❌ ~200 sf less space
  • ❌ May limit program flexibility

Recommendation: Evaluate whether the program can fit comfortably in <1,000 sf. If yes, the fee savings are substantial. If the extra space is essential for intended use or resale value, the higher fees may be justified.

Creek Types in Orinda

Creek classification determines setback requirements:

Creek Type Typical Setback Characteristics
Type 1 Larger setback (varies) Year-round flow, defined channel, riparian habitat
Type 2 Moderate setback (varies) Seasonal flow, v-ditch/swale, less defined
No Creek No creek setback Drainage feature only, no creek designation

Current Feature: V-ditch on property (photos submitted showing dry condition)

Which Creek Setback Matters?

Question: "Should we be focused on the creek setback that we will be eliminating or the creek setback that is between the two houses after being built?"

Answer: The setback that matters is the one between the existing creek/drainage feature and the proposed structures.

Explanation:

  1. You cannot "eliminate" a creek setback — if a creek or drainage feature exists and is classified by the city, setback requirements apply to any structures you build near it.
  2. The DPW site visit will determine:
    • Whether the v-ditch is classified as a creek (Type 1, Type 2, or not a creek)
    • Where the creek centerline/top of bank is located
    • What setback distance applies from that point
  3. The "between the two houses" concept doesn't apply here — this isn't about distance between buildings; it's about distance from the creek/drainage feature to your structures (main house, ADU, garage, patios, etc.).
  4. Setback is measured from the creek to structures, not between structures

Possible Outcomes

Outcome A: Not a Creek

  • V-ditch classified as drainage feature only
  • No creek setback required
  • ✅ Best case — no additional constraints

Outcome B: Type 2 Creek (Seasonal)

  • Moderate setback requirement (typically 10-30 feet from centerline)
  • May affect placement of structures near the v-ditch
  • 🟡 Manageable — site plan may need minor adjustments

Outcome C: Type 1 Creek (Year-Round)

  • Larger setback requirement (typically 30-50+ feet from top of bank)
  • Could significantly impact site plan
  • 🟡 More challenging — may require redesign

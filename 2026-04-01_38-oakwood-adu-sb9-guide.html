38 Oakwood Road — ADU, Second Unit & SB9 Guide

Project: Main House + 1,200 sf Accessory Unit + Garage  |  Orinda, CA

Prepared: April 1, 2026  |  For internal review with Drew

Overview

There are two separate questions to keep straight when thinking about the 1,200 sf accessory unit:

  1. How is it classified? ADU vs. Second Unit — affects fees only.
  2. Does it qualify for SB9 ministerial review? Separate question entirely — and the answer is yes, regardless of the ADU/Second Unit distinction.
Key Takeaway The ADU vs. Second Unit classification affects development fees — not the SB9 approval process. Both designations receive the same ministerial (staff-level, no public hearing) review under SB9.

Part 1 — ADU vs. Second Unit: What's the Difference?

What makes something an ADU?

Under California state law, an ADU is defined as a residential dwelling unit with complete independent living facilities — including permanent provisions for living, sleeping, eating, cooking, and sanitation. The full kitchen is what legally constitutes it as a dwelling unit.

California Government Code § 65852.2(a)(1)
"Accessory dwelling unit means an attached or a detached residential dwelling unit that provides complete independent living facilities for one or more persons… including permanent provisions for living, sleeping, eating, cooking, and sanitation."
leginfo.legislature.ca.gov → Gov Code § 65852.2

Orinda's local code mirrors this definition exactly:

Orinda Municipal Code § 17.3.4 — Accessory Dwelling Units
"An Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) is defined as 'an attached or a detached residential dwelling unit which provides complete independent living facilities for one or more persons.' By definition, an ADU includes permanent provisions for living, sleeping, eating, cooking, and sanitation."
cityoforinda.gov → Accessory Dwelling Units

Where does our 1,200 sf unit land?

Orinda caps the ADU size at 1,000 sf for units with two or more bedrooms. At 1,200 sf, the accessory unit exceeds that cap and is therefore classified as a Second Unit — not an ADU — under local code.

Orinda Municipal Code § 17.3.4 — Size Limits
"Maximum of 850 square-feet for a studio or one-bedroom. Maximum of 1,000 square-feet for more than one bedroom."
cityoforinda.gov → Accessory Dwelling Units
What the Second Unit designation changes Development fees only. The Second Unit classification forfeits the ADU impact fee discount, adding an estimated $20,000–$30,000 in development fees. All other rules — setbacks, height, approval process — remain the same.

Part 2 — SB9 and Why the ADU/Second Unit Split Doesn't Matter for Approval

What is SB9?

California Senate Bill 9 (effective January 1, 2022) requires all local governments to ministerially approve up to two residential dwelling units per parcel in single-family zones. Ministerial means: staff-level review only, no public hearing, no neighbor notification, no discretionary judgment.

California Government Code § 65852.21
"A proposed housing development containing no more than two residential units… shall be considered ministerially, without discretionary review or a hearing."
Gov Code § 65852.21 via Casetext

Orinda adopted SB9 via Ordinance 21-8 on January 11, 2022. The city confirmed at the February 9, 2026 pre-application meeting that our project qualifies for this path.

Orinda Ordinance 21-8 (Jan 11, 2022) / cityoforinda.gov SB9 Page
"Ministerial is defined as a decision that involves little or no personal judgment by a public official. This means that as long as the proposed project complies with all objective standards of the Orinda Municipal Code, the City must approve the project. There are no discretionary decisions made, no public hearings, and no neighborhood notification."
cityoforinda.gov → Senate Bill 9 (SB 9)

SB9 Units and ADUs are separate things

This is a common source of confusion. The city is explicit about it:

City of Orinda — SB9 FAQ
"An SB 9 unit is not the same as an Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU)."
cityoforinda.gov → Senate Bill 9 (SB 9)

SB9 applies to residential dwelling units broadly — not specifically to ADUs. Our 1,200 sf Second Unit is a residential dwelling unit with a full kitchen. It qualifies for SB9 ministerial review on equal footing with any ADU.

Part 4 — The Guesthouse Question

For completeness: a guesthouse (no full kitchen, not a legal dwelling unit) falls entirely outside SB9 and outside the ADU framework. It would be processed as a general accessory structure under Orinda's standard Design Review process — which does involve discretionary review and potential public hearings.

Because our accessory unit has a full kitchen and qualifies as a dwelling unit, this path does not apply. The full kitchen keeps it on the SB9 track and is the right call.

Why this matters SB9 ministerial review is the single most valuable permitting advantage on this project — it removes public hearings, neighbor appeals, and discretionary judgment from the zoning approval entirely. Keeping the full kitchen preserves that protection.

Bottom Line

  • Our 1,200 sf accessory unit has a full kitchen → it is a legal dwelling unit.
  • At 1,200 sf it is classified as a Second Unit (not ADU) under OMC § 17.3.4 — this adds ~$20–30K in development fees.
  • The Second Unit designation does not affect SB9. Ministerial approval, no public hearing, no neighbor notification — all still apply.
  • The only decision to revisit is whether the program can fit under 1,000 sf to recover the fee savings, without compromising livability.
